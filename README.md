Multi-Agent Generative AI Stock Market Simulation
Overview
This project is a Multi-Agent Stock Market Simulation powered by Generative AI, designed to study how autonomous AI traders interact within a virtual financial market. The simulation recreates realistic market behavior by combining multiple AI-driven trading agents with dynamic stock price movements, market news, liquidity constraints, and financial risk mechanisms. Through the interaction of diverse trading strategies and personalities, the system demonstrates emergent market phenomena such as volatility, trends, bubbles, and market shocks.
The virtual market consists of two tradable stocks, Stock A and Stock B, whose prices evolve dynamically based on supply-and-demand interactions generated by the agents. The simulation runs for a complete 264-day trading cycle, representing one trading year. During each trading day, agents independently decide whether to buy, sell, or hold assets based on their individual risk appetite, trading strategy, available capital, market conditions, and external news events.
The simulation includes 50 AI-powered trader agents, each uniquely configured with different personalities and behavioral traits. Agents may follow strategies such as momentum trading, contrarian investing, long-term investing, random trading, or news-driven decision-making. Generative AI reasoning enables agents to interpret market conditions differently, leading to realistic and unpredictable market dynamics.
To improve realism, the project incorporates advanced financial mechanics such as loans, leverage, interest accumulation, and bankruptcy conditions. Agents facing liquidity shortages can borrow capital, while highly leveraged traders become more vulnerable to bankruptcy during unfavorable market conditions. Agents unable to repay debt or maintain minimum capital requirements exit the market, impacting overall liquidity and volatility.
The simulation continuously tracks market activity, including stock prices, trading volume, portfolio values, loan balances, interest accumulation, and bankruptcy events. These outputs allow users to analyze market behavior, compare trading strategies, study emergent phenomena, and conduct research experiments in financial AI and behavioral economics.
